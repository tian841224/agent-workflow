# Humanizer pattern reference

Read only the groups that match the passage. These are signals to inspect, not automatic bans.

## Claims and framing

- Inflated importance, legacy, or broad impact for an ordinary fact.
- Name-dropping, vague experts, or unnamed reports used as proof.
- Formulaic challenge, future-outlook, or optimistic closing sections.
- Fake depth such as "at its core", "the real question", or a dramatic saying.
- Announcing the next point instead of stating it.
- Answering an objection nobody raised or rejecting a fake alternative.

State the concrete fact and its actor. Keep a named source, real objection, or real alternative when it adds information.

## Language and rhythm

- Stock AI words piled together: crucial, delve, enhance, foster, highlight, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant.
- Long substitutes for simple verbs such as "is", "has", and "uses".
- Repeated sentence openings, synonym cycling, forced groups of three, or even mid-length cadence.
- Passive voice that hides who acted.
- Too many qualifiers, clipped negative endings, and generic positive send-offs.
- Hyphenated word pairs used where normal grammar does not require them.

Prefer concrete verbs, varied sentence length, one clear name, and the amount of certainty the source supports.

## Formatting and chatbot residue

- Em or en dashes used as a repeated stylistic crutch. Remove them unless a writing sample uses them deliberately.
- Bold mini-headings, decorative emojis, title-case headings, or curly quotes that do not fit the target format.
- Greetings, offers, and closings such as "I hope this helps" left inside a standalone document.
- A heading followed by a sentence that merely repeats it.

Preserve correct formatting, quotes, headings, and sign-offs when they belong to the writer or target medium.

## False positives and voice

Do not rewrite solely because the prose is formal, polished, dry, uses one transition word, contains one dash, or includes a useful disclaimer. Keep specific details, dated references, mixed feelings, first-person choices, genuine asides, intentional rhythm, and quoted or discussed phrases. When uncertain, look for several signals together and preserve the source meaning.
